Privilege Zones help you expand identity attack path management beyond the default **Tier Zero** view. Use them to define the next protected boundary, analyze who can reach it, and decide where access violates your security model.

Tier Zero is the natural starting point because it contains identities, systems, and permissions that can control the broader environment. After you begin reducing attack paths to Tier Zero, use Privilege Zones to answer the next operational question: what do you protect next?

## Choose the next boundary

The next boundary depends on your environment and your business priorities. A Privilege Zone can represent a durable part of your operating model or a temporary focus area for a specific initiative.

Common examples include:

- A traditional administrative tier
- A critical application or regulated environment
- A business unit or ownership boundary
- A source code or deployment boundary
- An endpoint management boundary
- Another control plane that needs focused analysis

This approach helps your team turn broad security priorities into visible attack path boundaries. Instead of asking only whether an attacker can reach Tier Zero, you can ask whether lower-trust identities, systems, or platforms can reach the assets your organization has decided are most important.

## Use this approach when

- Your team has started reducing Tier Zero attack paths.
- You need a repeatable way to decide what to protect next.
- You want to connect attack path management to business priorities or ownership boundaries.
- You are adding more technologies into BloodHound and need focused analysis targets.

## Move from concept to configuration

Start with a practical first version of the boundary. You do not need a perfect model before you begin. A useful first version helps you validate assumptions, uncover unexpected control paths, and refine the zone over time.

After you choose a boundary:

- Create a [zone](/analyze-data/privilege-zones/zones) to represent the protected boundary.
- Use [labels](/analyze-data/privilege-zones/labels) to organize objects by owner, business function, campaign, or status.
- Define [rules](/analyze-data/privilege-zones/rules) to match the objects that belong in the zone or label.
