Privilege Zones help you expand identity Attack Path Management beyond the default **Tier Zero** view. Use them to define the next protected boundary, analyze who can reach it, and decide where access violates your security model.

Tier Zero is the natural starting point because it contains identities, systems, and permissions that can control the broader environment. After you begin reducing attack paths to Tier Zero, use Privilege Zones to answer the next operational question: what do you protect next?

The answer varies by organization. One team's next priority may be Tier One administrative systems. Another's may be a clinical application, a payment environment, a production deployment repository, an endpoint management platform, or a business-unit-owned infrastructure boundary.

## Choose the next boundary

The next boundary depends on your environment and your business priorities. A Privilege Zone can represent a durable part of your operating model or a temporary focus area for a specific initiative, such as a campaign, audit response, or focused investigation.

Common examples include:

- A traditional administrative tier
- A critical application
- A regulated environment
- A business unit
- A platform team
- A source code or deployment boundary
- An endpoint management boundary
- A technology control plane that needs focused analysis

This approach helps your team turn broad security priorities into visible attack path boundaries. Instead of asking only whether an attacker can reach Tier Zero, you can ask whether lower-trust identities, systems, or platforms can reach the assets your organization has decided are most important.

<Note>
  Treat Privilege Zones as living documents. Revisit and refine them as your environment changes, ownership becomes clearer, and your Attack Path Management program matures.
</Note>

## Use this approach when

- Your team has started reducing Tier Zero attack paths.
- Your team wants to expand Attack Path Management to additional critical systems.
- You need to organize remediation around business priorities, ownership boundaries, or regulatory scope.
- You need a repeatable way to decide what to protect next.
- You are adding OpenGraph-connected technologies and need to define which assets deserve focused analysis.

## Example progression

1. Start with Tier Zero and reduce the highest-impact attack paths.
1. Define a Tier One zone around administrative systems or critical infrastructure.
1. Define a zone around a specific business-critical application or regulated system.
1. Use labels or zones to map ownership across business units or platform teams.
1. Add OpenGraph-connected technologies, such as GitHub, Jamf, Okta, or cloud platforms.
1. Use zones to understand inherited risk across identity systems, endpoint management platforms, source code systems, and cloud environments.

## Move from concept to configuration

Start with a practical first version of the boundary. You do not need a perfect model before you begin. Many teams start with known groups, object IDs, organizational units, naming conventions, asset metadata, or simple Cypher queries, then use the results to validate assumptions, uncover unexpected control paths, and refine the zone over time.

Ask these questions to identify a starting point:

- What system would create the most business impact if compromised?
- What systems are subject to regulatory, audit, or compliance scrutiny?
- What administrative boundary do you need to validate after Tier Zero?
- Which application owners need visibility into inherited identity risk?
- Which business unit or platform team owns remediation?
- Which source code repositories, workflows, or endpoint management systems can influence production?
- What existing structure can help you build a first version of the zone?

You can use a broad naming pattern to discover candidate objects across your environment:

```cypher
MATCH (n:Base)
WHERE toUpper(COALESCE(n.name, '')) CONTAINS 'ADMIN'
   OR toUpper(COALESCE(n.name, '')) CONTAINS 'PRIV'
   OR toUpper(COALESCE(n.name, '')) CONTAINS 'TIER1'
   OR toUpper(COALESCE(n.name, '')) CONTAINS 'T1'
RETURN n
LIMIT 1000
```

Or narrow the search to a specific critical application by replacing `APPNAME` and `APPLICATION-ALIAS` with your application's name or alias:

```cypher
MATCH (n)
WHERE toUpper(COALESCE(n.name, '')) CONTAINS 'APPNAME'
   OR toUpper(COALESCE(n.name, '')) CONTAINS 'APPLICATION-ALIAS'
RETURN n
LIMIT 1000
```

After you choose a boundary:

- Create a [zone](/analyze-data/privilege-zones/zones) to represent the protected boundary.
- Use [labels](/analyze-data/privilege-zones/labels) to organize objects by owner, business function, campaign, or status.
- Define [rules](/analyze-data/privilege-zones/rules) to match the objects that belong in the zone or label.

Privilege Zones give teams a repeatable way to decide what to protect after Tier Zero and to keep expanding Attack Path Management as the environment matures.
